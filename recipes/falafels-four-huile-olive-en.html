Baked Falafel with olive oil

Golden oven-baked falafel, lighter than fried but just as tasty. The secret is one detail: soaked dried chickpeas (never canned), blended raw with herbs and spices, then roasted with olive oil.

Prep: 20 min (+12 h soaking) · Cook: 30 min · Serves: 4 · Cuisine: Middle Eastern · Difficulty: easy

Good falafel is not about frying but about the base: dried chickpeas, soaked then blended raw. Canned (already cooked) chickpeas make a wet paste that falls apart. Baked with a good drizzle of olive oil, you get a golden crust and a soft centre, with no deep-frying.

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried chickpeas (soaked 12 h, uncooked)
  • 1 onion
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 bunch flat-leaf parsley
  • 1 bunch cilantro (coriander)
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1 tbsp flour (if needed)
  • Salt, pepper, extra virgin olive oil for brushing

Step-by-step method

  1. Soak. The night before, cover the dried chickpeas generously with cold water and soak 12 h. Drain: they will be raw, that's normal.
  2. Blend. Blend the chickpeas with onion, garlic, parsley, cilantro, cumin, ground coriander, salt and pepper. Aim for a sandy, even texture that holds when pressed — not a smooth purée.
  3. Rest. Add the baking soda (and flour if too sticky), mix and rest 30 min in the fridge.
  4. Shape. Form balls or small patties about 4 cm. Place on a lined baking tray.
  5. Bake. Brush generously with olive oil. Bake at 200 °C (fan) for 25–30 min, turning halfway, until well browned.
  6. Serve. Serve hot with tahini sauce, pita bread, crudités and a drizzle of olive oil.

The role of olive oil

In the oven, olive oil replaces the fryer: it browns the surface and adds moisture. Brush the falafel before and, if needed, halfway. A fruity extra virgin boosts flavour without weighing it down. A raw drizzle on the plate lifts everything.

Tips for success

  • DRIED soaked chickpeas, never canned: the anti-crumble rule.
  • Don't blend to a smooth purée: a grainy texture holds better.
  • Rest the mixture 30 min before shaping.
  • Brush well with olive oil for a golden oven crust.

Variations

  • Pan-fried: brown the patties in a little olive oil, 3–4 min per side.
  • Green version: more herbs for a bright green inside.
  • Spicy: a touch of chili or harissa in the mix.

How to serve it

Serve falafel in a pita with tahini sauce, tomato, cucumber and salad, as a mezze with hummus and grilled vegetables, or in a bowl with grains and crudités. A drizzle of olive oil and a squeeze of lemon at serving make the difference.

Storage

They keep 3 days in the fridge and reheat in the oven to regain their crunch. Both the raw mix and cooked falafel freeze very well: reheat straight from the oven.

Frequently asked questions

Can you make falafel with canned chickpeas?

Not recommended: canned chickpeas are already cooked and give a wet paste that crumbles when cooked. Use dried chickpeas soaked 12 h and uncooked.

How do I stop falafel falling apart?

Use soaked dried chickpeas, don't blend to a smooth purée, rest the mix, and add a little flour if too wet. Baking soda also helps them hold.

Baked or fried falafel?

Fried is crunchier; baked is lighter with no oil bath. Brushed with olive oil and baked at 200 °C, oven falafel browns very nicely.

How long do you bake falafel?

About 25–30 minutes at 200 °C (fan), turning halfway, until golden brown.

What sauce goes with falafel?

Tahini sauce (sesame paste, lemon, garlic, water) is the classic. Garlic-mint yogurt or an olive-oil-and-lemon dressing also work well.
