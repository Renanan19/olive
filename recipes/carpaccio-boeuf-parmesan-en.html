Beef Carpaccio with olive oil

Carpaccio, born at Harry's Bar in Venice, is raw beef sliced into translucent sheets, dressed simply with extra virgin olive oil, lemon, Parmesan and capers. Success comes down to the freshness of the meat and the thinness of the cut.

Prep: 20 min (+40 min freezer) · Cook: 0 min · Serves: 4 (starter) · Cuisine: Italian · Difficulty: medium

A good carpaccio asks almost nothing of the cook but everything of the shopping: very fresh meat, a sharp blade and a characterful olive oil. The classic Italian version needs only lemon, olive oil, shaved Parmesan and arugula — the opposite of a heavy sauce.

Ingredients

  • 300 g very fresh beef fillet (or eye of round)
  • 4 tbsp fruity extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 lemon (juice)
  • 40 g shaved Parmigiano Reggiano
  • 1 handful arugula (rocket)
  • 1 tbsp capers
  • Freshly ground black pepper, flaky salt

Step-by-step method

  1. Firm the meat. Freeze the fillet for 30–45 minutes: firm but not frozen, it slices far thinner.
  2. Slice thin. With a very sharp knife, cut the thinnest slices you can. To thin them further, place each slice between two sheets of film and gently flatten with a rolling pin or the flat of a knife.
  3. Plate. Lay the slices in a single layer on a cold plate, covering the whole base.
  4. Dress. Whisk the extra virgin olive oil with the lemon juice and a pinch of salt; spoon over the meat. Add pepper, shaved Parmesan, arugula and capers.
  5. Serve at once. Carpaccio is eaten immediately, well chilled, with bread or grissini.

The role of olive oil

On raw meat, olive oil is the main seasoning: it coats, softens and lifts. Choose a fruity, slightly peppery extra virgin: its elegant bitterness balances the richness of the beef and wakes the whole plate. Add it just before serving.

Tips for success

  • Very fresh, high-quality meat from a trusted butcher: rule number one for anything raw.
  • Firm in the freezer before cutting = thinner slices.
  • Cold plate and immediate service: carpaccio does not wait.
  • Season at the last moment: lemon 'cooks' and greys the meat if added too early.

Variations

  • Harry's Bar sauce: a few drops of creamy lemon sauce instead of the oil-lemon dressing.
  • Other carpaccios: same idea with sea bream, tuna, scallops or, plant-based, thinly sliced courgette or beetroot.

How to serve it

Serve carpaccio as a chilled starter, dressed with olive oil at the last moment, with toasted bread, grissini or a small arugula salad. Iced water and a lemon on the side let everyone adjust the acidity.

Storage

Carpaccio is made and eaten the same day. Sliced raw meat does not keep: refrigerate until serving and never leave it at room temperature. Not recommended for pregnant women or immunocompromised people (raw meat).

Frequently asked questions

Which cut for beef carpaccio?

A lean, tender, very fresh cut: beef fillet ideally, or eye of round. Buy it whole from a trusted butcher and slice it yourself on the day.

Is carpaccio raw? Is it safe?

Yes, it is raw meat. The risk is controlled with very fresh, high-quality meat kept cold and served immediately. It remains inadvisable for pregnant women, young children and immunocompromised people.

How do I slice carpaccio very thin?

Firm the meat for 30–45 minutes in the freezer, use a very sharp knife, then flatten each slice between two sheets of film if needed.

Olive oil and lemon, or a sauce?

The classic Italian version uses extra virgin olive oil and lemon with Parmesan and arugula. The original Harry's Bar version used a creamy lemon sauce — it is a matter of taste.

Can carpaccio be prepared ahead?

You can slice the meat a few hours ahead and keep it cold between films, but dress it only at serving time, otherwise the lemon greys it.
