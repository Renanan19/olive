Cold-Pressed Olive Oil: benefits and what it really means

'Cold-pressed' is on every premium olive oil label. Here's what it actually means, why it matters for polyphenols and flavour, and where the term is really just marketing.

Cold-pressed (more accurately, cold-extracted) olive oil is oil produced without heat. Heat would raise yield but degrade the delicate polyphenols and aromas. This guide explains what the term really means, the health angle, and how to choose — without the hype.

Key facts
  • Cold extraction means the oil is produced below about 27 °C, preserving heat-sensitive polyphenols and aromas.
  • It is these polyphenols (hydroxytyrosol, oleocanthal) that carry the antioxidant benefits linked to olive oil.
  • Most genuine extra virgin olive oil is already cold-extracted, so the label is often redundant.
  • What matters most is that the oil is real extra virgin, fresh, and well stored — more than the 'cold-pressed' wording.

What 'cold-pressed' really means

Historically, olives were pressed; today most oil is separated by centrifuge. 'Cold-pressed' or, in EU terms, 'cold extraction', means the process stays below about 27 °C. Heat increases the amount of oil extracted but degrades the fragile polyphenols and aromatic compounds — so quality producers keep it cold.

Why it matters for health

The health interest of olive oil rests largely on its polyphenols, which are heat-sensitive. Cold extraction preserves more of them, along with vitamin E and aroma. These polyphenols underlie the EU-authorised claim that olive oil polyphenols help protect blood lipids from oxidative stress. In that sense, cold-extracted oil retains more of what makes olive oil beneficial.

The honest catch: it's often redundant

Here's what labels won't tell you: genuine extra virgin olive oil is, by definition, already cold-extracted and mechanically produced. So 'cold-pressed' on an extra virgin bottle is frequently a marketing repetition rather than an extra guarantee. It does not, on its own, prove higher quality.

How to actually choose a good oil

Rather than chase the 'cold-pressed' label, look for: the words extra virgin, a recent harvest date, protective dark glass, an origin you can trace, and a peppery, fresh taste. A fresh, well-stored extra virgin olive oil delivers the polyphenol benefits whether or not the front label repeats 'cold-pressed'.

Frequently asked questions

Is cold-pressed olive oil better?

Cold extraction preserves more polyphenols and aroma than hot processing, so it is preferable in principle. But genuine extra virgin olive oil is already cold-extracted, so the label alone doesn't guarantee superiority — freshness and real extra virgin quality matter more.

What are the benefits of cold-pressed olive oil?

By avoiding heat, it retains more polyphenols (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory compounds), vitamin E and aroma. These are the components linked to olive oil's health benefits within a balanced diet.

Is cold-pressed olive oil healthy?

Yes, as part of a balanced diet. It is a good source of monounsaturated fat and polyphenols. Its benefits come over time, not from any single dose.

Is cold-pressed the same as extra virgin?

Not exactly. 'Extra virgin' is a graded quality category; 'cold-pressed' describes the low-temperature process. In practice, real extra virgin oil is already cold-extracted, so the two usually go together.

Can you cook with cold-pressed olive oil?

Yes. Like any extra virgin olive oil, it is stable enough for most home cooking. To preserve its heat-sensitive polyphenols, keep some for raw finishing.
