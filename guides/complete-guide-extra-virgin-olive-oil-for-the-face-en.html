Extra Virgin Olive Oil for the Face

Used in cosmetics since antiquity, extra virgin olive oil nourishes and protects the skin. Here are its uses on the face, its active compounds, and the cases where it's better avoided.

Extra virgin olive oil is a natural emollient rich in protective compounds. On the face it can moisturise, remove makeup and soften — but it doesn't suit every skin type. Here's an honest guide, benefits and limits included.

Key facts
  • Olive oil contains squalene, oleic acid, vitamin E and polyphenols with emollient and antioxidant properties.
  • Common uses: makeup remover, cleansing oil, moisturising treatment, massage oil.
  • High in oleic acid, it can weaken the skin barrier in some sensitive or acne-prone skin: patch-test first.
  • It replaces neither a sunscreen nor a dermatological treatment.

Why olive oil is used on skin

Extra virgin olive oil is an emollient: it forms a film that limits water loss and softens the skin. It provides squalene (close to a natural skin lipid), vitamin E and antioxidant polyphenols. This composition explains its traditional use in Mediterranean cosmetics.

Makeup removal and oil cleansing

Like any oil, olive oil dissolves makeup and sebum: massage a few drops onto dry skin, then wipe off with a warm damp cloth. This is the basis of 'oil cleansing'. The result is gentle, but rinse well so you don't leave a film on combination-to-oily skin.

Moisturising and care

In very small amounts, olive oil can seal in moisture on dry skin, on the body and on rough areas (elbows, heels). On the face, one or two drops are enough, ideally on still-damp skin to trap water.

Who it doesn't suit

An important and often-omitted point: olive oil is high in oleic acid, and studies suggest it can weaken the skin barrier in some people, particularly sensitive, atopic or acne-prone skin. It can be comedogenic for acne-prone skin. Always patch-test on a small area, and avoid on broken skin without medical advice.

How to use it well

Choose a quality extra virgin olive oil, in small amounts, and test first on the inner arm. Favour evening use (makeup removal, treatment), not as sun protection. If in doubt or if you have a skin condition, ask a dermatologist.

Frequently asked questions

Is olive oil good for the face?

It can moisturise, remove makeup and soften thanks to its squalene and vitamin E. But it doesn't suit all skin: high in oleic acid, it can weaken the barrier of some sensitive or acne-prone skin.

Can you remove makeup with olive oil?

Yes. Massage a few drops onto dry skin to dissolve makeup, then wipe off with a warm damp cloth and rinse. It's a gentle, economical makeup remover.

Is olive oil comedogenic?

It can be for acne-prone skin, because of its high oleic acid content. If you have oily or acne-prone skin, test carefully or prefer a lighter oil.

Which olive oil for skin?

A quality extra virgin olive oil, fresh and well stored. Refining removes some polyphenols; extra virgin keeps more.

Does olive oil protect against the sun?

No. It doesn't replace sunscreen and offers no reliable UV protection. Use a dedicated sunscreen.

Informational cosmetic use. Patch-test on a small area first and consult a dermatologist if you have sensitive, acne-prone or problem skin.
