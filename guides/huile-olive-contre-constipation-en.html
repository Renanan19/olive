Olive Oil for Constipation

A spoonful of olive oil for constipation is a traditional remedy. It has a rationale — oil lubricates and stimulates digestion — but also limits. Here's how to use it sensibly.

Olive oil is among the most cited home remedies for occasional constipation. It acts as a mild lubricant and stimulates the gallbladder, which can ease transit. Evidence is limited, but the practice is simple and generally low-risk in small amounts.

Key facts
  • Olive oil acts as a gentle lubricant and stimulates bile secretion, which can soften stool and ease transit.
  • Common use: 1 tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil, often on an empty stomach in the morning, sometimes with a little lemon juice.
  • Small studies (notably in dialysis patients) found an effect comparable to mineral oil on constipation.
  • It is not a treatment for chronic constipation: water, fibre and physical activity remain the basis.

Why olive oil can help

Olive oil provides fats that lubricate the intestinal contents and make stool more slippery. It also stimulates bile secretion, which aids fat digestion and transit. For occasional constipation, a small dose can therefore give a helpful nudge.

How to use it

The most common way: 1 tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil, in the morning on an empty stomach, optionally mixed with a little lemon juice. Some take it in the evening. Drink water and include it within a fibre-rich diet (fruit, vegetables, whole grains).

What the studies say

The data are limited but encouraging. Trials in haemodialysis patients, who are often prone to constipation, showed olive oil could improve stool consistency and frequency, comparably to mineral oil. In the general population, the effect is plausible but less documented.

Limits and when to see a doctor

Olive oil is not a strong laxative or a root-cause treatment. One tablespoon is ~120 kcal, worth noting. See a doctor if constipation is chronic, painful, newly appeared and unexplained, or comes with blood, weight loss or vomiting. Do not give oil to an infant for this purpose without paediatric advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does olive oil really help with constipation?

It can help with occasional constipation: it lubricates the intestinal contents and stimulates bile. Small studies suggest a real effect, comparable to mineral oil, but it is not a strong laxative.

How much olive oil for constipation?

Usually 1 tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil, often on an empty stomach in the morning. There's no need for more: beyond that, you mainly add calories.

Should you take olive oil on an empty stomach for transit?

Many take it in the morning on an empty stomach, sometimes with lemon, as an empty stomach may favour the effect. There's no strict evidence that timing fundamentally changes the result.

Does olive oil and lemon work for constipation?

The mix is popular: the oil lubricates, the lemon adds freshness and a little acidity. The effect comes mainly from the oil; the lemon mostly improves the taste.

When should you see a doctor for constipation?

If it is chronic, painful, newly appeared and unexplained, or comes with blood, fever, vomiting or weight loss. A doctor should then assess the cause.

General information, not medical advice. For a health problem, an ongoing treatment or an infant, consult a doctor or pharmacist.
